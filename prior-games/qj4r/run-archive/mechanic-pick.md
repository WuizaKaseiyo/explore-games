# Mechanic pick — qj4r

## ID
`qj4r` — 4-char lowercase alphanumeric. Not in the 25 reference IDs. Not in `prior-games/index.md` (37 priors). Not in the prior-games directory (incl. fb7t). Not an English word. Opaque per §3.4.

## Mechanic family
`fold-mirror-pair`

## Description (one paragraph)
The active playfield is a coloured rectangular sheet centred inside the 64×64 frame; it holds a small set of coloured "dot" pieces and same-coloured "ring" targets, plus (at L2/L3) lock cells and obstacle posts. A cardinal arrow press *folds* the sheet along its central horizontal or vertical axis: the pressed-direction half flips over the opposite half (each dot on the folded-out half reflects to the mirrored cell of the kept half, like creasing a piece of paper), and the folded-out half is permanently retired into the dim padding region — the active playable area shrinks by half each fold. The level wins when every dot occupies the cell of its same-coloured ring target; the level loses when the step counter empties. The verbs are reflection + region-contraction (geometry/topology prior) acting on movable dots (objectness prior); each fold composes with prior folds, and the player plans a chain of fold directions that routes every dot onto its target.

## Closest taxonomy near-misses + distinguishing rules
- **ar25 — shape-mirror-cover.** ar25 has a permanent straight mirror-line floating on the playfield AND a single shape that always projects a continuously-updating ghost across the line; arrows nudge the shape or the line, and the ghost walks across scattered dots until covering them. Distinguishing rule: in qj4r there is no permanent mirror line and no continuous ghost — the fold is a *one-shot* spatial reflection that *also contracts the playfield* (the folded-out half is permanently padded out); ar25's playfield never shrinks and pieces never disappear from one half. The dynamic the player thinks about is "which fold direction routes pieces to targets in this contracted geometry?", not "where do I walk the shape so its mirror image covers the dots?".
- **m0r0 — mirror-orb-merge.** m0r0 mirrors *avatar motion*: two pawns are mirror-twins, and one direction press moves both with axis-flipped semantics. Distinguishing rule: qj4r mirrors the *playfield itself*, not pawn motion — each fold reflects all pieces simultaneously across a single fold line and then halves the field. There is no notion of "twin pawns moving with flipped semantics"; the pieces are fixed during a non-fold action (which doesn't exist) and reflected once-per-fold.
- **cn04 — rotate-translate-jigsaw.** cn04 rotates a *single selected piece* 90° in place; pieces shift one-cell-at-a-time on arrows. Distinguishing rule: qj4r has no piece-selection step, no per-piece rotation, no per-piece arrow translation; the verb acts on the entire sheet at once.
- **bx84 — beam-mirror-reflect (prior).** bx84 fires a beam that reflects off click-placed mirror sprites. Distinguishing rule: qj4r has no projectile / no beam, and no per-cell mirror sprites; the "mirror" is the central fold axis of the entire sheet, applied as a one-shot transform of every piece.
- **wt39 — glide-deflect-thaw (prior).** wt39 glides a single pawn until it hits a wall or bumper. Distinguishing rule: qj4r's pieces don't glide — a fold reflects every piece across the central axis simultaneously and then contracts the field; the wall isn't what stops motion, the fold axis is.
- **tg6w — settle-pile-tilt (prior).** tg6w tilts the playfield's gravity direction; loose blocks slide multi-cell to settle against rim walls. Distinguishing rule: qj4r doesn't slide or settle anything — pieces *teleport* via reflection across the fold line, and the playfield itself shrinks per fold (tg6w's playfield is fixed-size).
- **pz4t — anchor-pivot-place (prior).** pz4t tiles a region with components placed by anchor-click + reflect/rotate. Distinguishing rule: qj4r has no tiling, no manual placement, and no anchor-click — the action is a global fold of the active sheet, and pieces' destinations are *forced* by the reflection geometry, not chosen.
- **qm4t — convex-pen-trap (prior).** qm4t drops vertex posts via clicks; their convex hull defines a pen. Distinguishing rule: completely different action verb (click-place posts vs. arrow-fold-sheet), different geometric primitive (convex hull vs. central-axis reflection + contraction).
