**`polarity-attract-discharge`**

## One-paragraph mechanic description

The board hosts a small population of "polarity pawns" — square sprites whose interior pattern encodes one of two binary states (call them *yang* / top-half-filled and *yin* / bottom-half-filled). The player has only two verbs: ACTION6 (click any pawn to flip its state from yang↔yin), and ACTION5 (advance one global "attract tick"). On every attract tick, every pawn computes its nearest opposite-state pawn by Manhattan distance and steps one cell toward it along the dominant axis (with walls blocking and ties broken deterministically by axis-then-direction priority). When a yang pawn and a yin pawn end up on adjacent cells (4-neighbour) they **discharge** — both are removed from the board. The level wins when every pawn has discharged. The whole game is therefore a planning puzzle over the *order* of polarity-flips and attract-ticks: the player must pair pawns by flipping (so each yang has a unique yin partner), then run the simulation, repairing the pairing whenever the wrong pair is about to dock first. Levels add new mechanics: L2 introduces **colour-keyed discharge** (each pawn carries a ring colour; discharge only fires when a yang and a yin of *the same ring colour* meet — wrong-colour collisions just block movement), and L3 introduces **flip-pads** (cells that automatically flip the polarity of any pawn that passes through them, turning the level into a routing problem where the player must choose paths so attract-walks pass through the right number of pads).

## Core knowledge priors used

| Prior | How it's used |
|---|---|
| Objectness | Pawns are coherent persistent objects that occupy single cells, are pushed by attraction, and disappear on discharge. |
| Agentness | Each pawn is a small intent-bearing agent; on every tick it pursues a target (its nearest opposite-state pawn). |
| Basic geometry & topology | Yang/yin are encoded as topologically distinct interior patterns (top-half vs bottom-half fill — asymmetric vertical orientation), and L2's colour-keyed discharge plus L3's flip-pads add topology to the routing problem. |
| Basic physics | The attract-tick is intuitive "things attract their opposite, like little magnets"; one Manhattan-step per tick gives a deterministic, learnable physics. |

Three of four priors load-bearing; physics carries the dynamics, agentness carries the per-pawn target choice, objectness/topology give the discharge predicate. No fourth-prior dependency.

## Action enum

`available_actions = [5, 6]` — pure ACTION5 + ACTION6 game. Slots 1-4 (cardinal motion) and 7 (undo) are deliberately not exposed: the player has no manual nudge of any individual pawn, which is the load-bearing constraint that forces planning around the attract-tick. Random-policy resistance comes directly from this minimalism — flipping random pawns and ticking will not produce the right pairing under any plausible random sequence past L1.

## Action-budget sanity (RHAE-aware)

L3 with flip-pads + walls around 60-90 actions. The 5× human-baseline budget bound is therefore comfortable inside a couple-hundred-step level cap.
